Installing it

Open the disk image and drag vdodtor onto the Applications alias beside it. That is the whole installation; there is nothing else to run, and nothing is put anywhere else on your Mac.

The Licences folder on the disk image holds the notices for the open-source software vdodtor carries, so you can read them before installing rather than after. They are also in the app's About panel and at this address.

There is no updater, and that is on purpose

vdodtor has no network entitlement, so it cannot check for a new version any more than it can send your footage anywhere. Both come from the same decision, and the second is worth more than the first.

So nothing will ever nag you, and nothing will ever tell you. When you want to know, come back here: the version above is the current one, and the app's About panel shows the one you have. That version line is selectable, so it can be pasted straight into a bug report.

What you get for nothing

The whole editor, exporting up to 1080p, with no watermark and no account. That is not a trial. Pro adds 4K export and premium content packs, and buying it is the only thing on this site that asks you for an email address.

The first launch

The first window offers you a sample project — a short edit that is already cut, graded and captioned — beside New Project, because an empty timeline teaches nobody anything. A six-stop tour runs over the real panels the first time you open an editor window; you can skip it, and it is in the keyboard-shortcut sheet afterwards.
